# The Underdwellers — A Homebrew Player Race

*A pillworm-like, invertebrate, subterranean tool-using species. Built as an alternative to traditional "short human" dwarves — no vision, no spoken language, and a genuinely different way of experiencing the world both underground and on the surface.*

## Concept & Physiology

- Roughly 1.5 m tall, segmented, isopod/pillworm-like body plan with a chitinous exoskeleton.
- **Lower limbs**: multiple pairs, dedicated to locomotion.
- **Upper limbs**: four pairs of thin, arthropod-like limbs — closer in function to fingers than to hands or arms. Single pairs are dexterous and precise (fine manipulation, delicate work) but individually weak; anything requiring real force or a two-handed weapon needs most or all pairs engaged together.
- **Head**: no eyes in the human sense — only vestigial photoreceptors (light/dark only, no image formation) and several long, mobile antennae used for smell, taste, and fine tactile sensing.
- No lungs/larynx analog for producing human speech; no ears for airborne sound.

## Senses

**Tremorsense (primary, tiered by range)**
- **0–60 ft.**: Precise. Anything in contact with the ground/a connected solid surface is located exactly.
- **61–120 ft.**: Vague. General direction and rough distance only, for creatures Medium+ or moving forcefully; disadvantage on attacks based only on this; small/stealthy movement doesn't register.
- **120+ ft.**: Only very large-scale events (explosions, collapses) register, as a "something happened, that direction" cue.

**Scent (secondary, for anything not grounded)**
- Flying, waterborne, or otherwise non-grounded creatures are invisible to tremorsense but detectable by antenna-scent within ~30 ft. (wind-affected). Tells you *presence* and a rough bearing only — never precise location. Scentless creatures (constructs, some undead, oozes) give nothing away at all.

**Antenna Probe (close-range precision, risk/reward)**
- As a bonus action, extend antennae to get full precise information on one creature/object within 5 ft. (one body length) — resolves any "vague" or "scent-only" read into certainty.
- Risk: probing something you haven't already precisely located (e.g., a flying creature you only smell) provokes a free reaction attack from it — you're reaching blind into range. Creates a constant tactical choice: advance and confirm, or hold back and act on inference.

**Vestigial photoreceptors**: light/dark awareness only, no shapes; effectively blind for all sight-based tasks.

**Sunlight Sensitivity**: disadvantage on attacks and other-sense-based Perception checks in direct sunlight (photoreceptor overload/glare, not blindness).

## Communication

- **Kin-tongue**: a chemo-tactile "first language," antenna-to-antenna, close range only. Biologically impossible for non-antennae species to learn or perceive.
- **Undercant**: a secondary tap/scratch vibration language, usable at distance through solid surfaces (rock, floors, walls). Because it's inherently discrete/digital (unlike the chemical language), it's also the basis of their **writing system** — words are physically encoded as grooves/notches (frozen taps), read by dragging an antenna across the surface. Non-linear scripts (spirals, tunnel-shaped) are plausible given their spatial culture.
- Cannot produce or perceive spoken Common or other airborne language without external help.

## Society & Culture

**Clan-holds, not cities.** Because Kin-tongue only works antenna-to-antenna at close range, Underdweller social bonds are necessarily physical and tight-knit — a "clan-hold" is a cluster of connected chambers where kin live close enough to touch daily, not a sprawling city of strangers. Larger settlements exist, but function more like several clan-holds sharing tunnel infrastructure (forges, mine shafts, water) than a single unified populace — trust and true intimacy stop at the edge of who you've physically touched. An Underdweller among strangers, even other Underdwellers from a different hold, is in a real sense among foreigners until proximity and time build a bond.

**The Wall-Debt (memory-as-law).** This is the institution that makes them read as *dwarves* rather than just "well-designed cave invertebrates." Every clan-hold maintains a **Reckoning Wall** — a stretch of stone near the hearth-chamber where wrongs, debts, oaths, and grievances are physically notched in Undercant, carved to last. A debt unpaid, a kinsman betrayed, an oath broken: it goes on the wall, permanently, in the same medium as their writing and their history. Nothing is forgotten because nothing *can* be — the notch is stone, not memory, and stone doesn't fade or get misremembered. Reading the wall before certain rites (coming of age, marriage, taking on an apprentice, leaving for the surface) is a Kin-tongue social obligation: you run your antennae over your family's notches so you carry the ledger with you, even into a world that can't read it. A grudge against an individual can pass down for generations exactly the way a dwarven grudge might in more familiar fantasy — the difference is it's not oral legend, it's a literal, sensable, unforgeable groove in the rock, which makes it very hard to talk your way out of and very easy for a DM to use as a plot hook ("the wall in your home-hold still bears your grandmother's debt to the humans who never came back").

**Explicit theme — permanence-lovers punished by an impermanent world.** Classic dwarves resist change and suffer for the resistance (hoarding, isolationism, grudges that calcify into ruin). Underdwellers invert the mechanism but land on the same wound: everything about them — their writing, their law, their sense of identity — is built on the idea that some things should be *permanent and unchangeable*, carved once and true forever. The surface breaks that premise immediately. Up there, nothing stays notched: sound doesn't hold still to be read, meaning has to be caught in real time and lost again, a prosthesis fails in the rain, comprehension has to be painstakingly rebuilt session by session instead of being carved once and settled. Their tragic flaw isn't stubbornness — it's that they've built an entire civilization on permanence, and the overground world simply does not hold still long enough for them to carve anything into it. Worth stating this outright in table fluff rather than leaving it implicit, so a player's slow, halting adaptation reads as *the* dwarvish flaw in alien form, not as an incidental disability mechanic.

## Magic & Communication

**Telepathy is the one clean workaround — and it's precious to them because of it.** Mind-to-mind magic (an innate telepathy trait, *Rary's Telepathic Bond*, the *Telepathy* spell, *Sending*) transmits thought directly, bypassing every sense entirely — no tremorsense, no antennae, no ears. The standard rule for these effects is that the target doesn't need to share your language, only to understand *some* language of its own for the meaning to land — and since Undercant and Kin-tongue both count, an Underdweller clears that bar easily. A human wizard with the right spell could hold a full, fluent conversation with an Underdweller with no translator and no prosthesis at all.

That makes telepathy-granting magic disproportionately valuable to this species — arguably a treasured relic rather than a curiosity, since it's the only known method giving them unmediated, full-fidelity contact with a surface-dweller. Good adventure/quest-reward material.

**What it doesn't replicate**: telepathy conveys the *meaning* of words, not the sensory experience of Kin-tongue itself — the antenna-contact, the pheromone nuance. Even perfect telepathic fluency with a human would still feel like talking through glass compared to talking with kin, which nicely preserves Kin-tongue's intimacy even in a setting where telepathy exists.

**Comprehend Languages / Tongues**, by contrast, are keyed to spoken or written language and likely wouldn't crack Undercant or Kin-tongue without a specific DM ruling — worth deciding at session zero whether Undercant (vibration through stone rather than sound through air) counts as "spoken" for that spell's purposes.

## Diet & Metabolism

**Omnivorous, but ectothermic — and that changes the whole economics of eating.** As cold-blooded invertebrates, Underdwellers don't burn calories generating their own body heat, and likely run a lower baseline metabolism than a similarly-sized mammal to begin with. The upshot: they need dramatically less food than a human of comparable size, and can go long stretches — plausibly days at a time — between meals without harm.

**What they eat.** Real caves have no sunlight to power a food web, so their diet is built on the two things that actually work underground:
- **Cultivated chemosynthetic mats** — bacteria grown in warm, mineral-rich seep chambers, their equivalent of a wheat field. Raw mat is mildly toxic; cooking (fire, already in hand) is what makes it safely edible, so cooking isn't decorative, it's a survival step.
- **Fungiculture on imported detritus** — mushrooms and molds grown on guano, driftwood, and leaf litter washed or blown in from the surface; their "vegetable" tier.
- **Cave livestock**: blind fish, cave shrimp/crayfish, cave crickets, farmed or trapped, plus bat-roosts kept for guano (fertilizer) and occasionally meat.
- **Minerals eaten directly** — geophagy, using the same antennae-taste already used for ore-prospecting; sometimes "checking a vein" and "having a snack" are the same motion.
- **Surface imports** (grain, dried fruit, preserved meat) as rare luxury goods, valued the way salt or spice once was for real inland trade routes — precious specifically because, unlike everything else on this list, they spoil.

**Food as ceremony, not routine.** If a single meal can sustain someone for the better part of a week, eating stops being a daily habit and becomes an *event* — probably the single best cultural fit for everything else you've built. A clan-hold might genuinely gather only once a week (or less) for a full communal meal, and that gathering becomes the natural venue for reading the Reckoning Wall aloud, swearing oaths, binding an apprentice, settling a debt in front of witnesses — food, law, and family all collapsed into the same rare occasion, rather than three separate institutions. "Sunday lunch with the family" isn't a quaint aside for this species — it may be *the* central recurring institution their whole social calendar is organized around.

**Adventuring practicalities.** An Underdweller party member likely doesn't need daily rations the way human companions do — a small, constant, humanizing reminder of their alienness ("you haven't eaten in four days and you're fine?"), and a minor logistics quirk if the DM tracks rations (lighter food load, or a standing joke that they've been quietly living off one meal since the party left town).

## Overground Adaptations (why they need a party)

- **Deaf to airborne sound** by default — speech just washes over their exoskeleton with nothing to catch it.
- **Translator option**: a companion who's learned Undercant can tap a real-time translation directly against the dwarf's forearm.
- **Sonic Prosthesis**: a crude, homemade mechanical "eardrum" (membrane + horn + stylus against the skin) that converts airborne sound into perceivable vibration. Functional but limited — no directionality, poor in wind/rain, and delivers raw noise rather than meaning; comprehension of actual words is a slow, learnable skill over time (cochlear-implant-style adaptation), a nice long-term character arc hook.
- Scent-tracking still works outdoors, but shifts from underground's precise "stereo" gradient-reading to coarser open-air "casting" (zigzagging across a windborne scent trail, moth-style).
- Balance/orientation sense (if included) works identically above and below ground — genuinely excellent footing on cliffs, rubble, ledges.

## Proposed 5e Racial Traits

- **ASI**: Str +2, Con +1
- **Size**: Medium; treated as one size larger for carrying/lifting (Powerful Build)
- **Speed**: 25 ft. (30 ft. underground/in contact with stone on 2+ sides, plus advantage on initiative in that terrain — "Tunnel Fighter")
- **Tremorsense**: as tiered above (replaces darkvision — no darkvision)
- **Scent**: as above
- **Antenna Probe**: bonus action, as above
- **No Airborne Hearing**: auto-fail hearing-based checks unless via solid-surface contact or a Sonic Prosthesis (disadvantage on hearing checks, disadvantage on locating sound direction)
- **Vestigial Sight**: light/dark only; effectively blind otherwise
- **Sunlight Sensitivity**
- **Many-Limbed (flavor only, no mechanical benefit)**: four pairs of thin, dexterous, finger-like upper limbs — closer in function to fingers than to separate arms. A two-handed weapon or heavy tool realistically requires most or all of them working together; a single pair alone is precise but not strong. Fine for roleplay and physical description (delicate tasks with a few limbs, all eight bracing a boulder), but doesn't grant extra attacks, extra held items, or grapple/disarm advantage — the strength budget is the same as any other Medium creature, just distributed differently.
- **Stonecunning**: double proficiency on History checks about stonework
- **Languages**: Undercant (tap/vibration) and Kin-tongue (touch/chemical, only usable with others who share the trait); no spoken languages without a translator/prosthesis

## Class Notes

No class is off-limits. Highlights:
- **Barbarian/Fighter/Monk**: strong physical fit — Str +2/Con +1 and Powerful Build support a heavy melee build; the multi-limbed grip is flavor (bracing a weapon with all eight limbs for a mighty swing) rather than a mechanical bonus
- **Rogue**: powerful underground (tremorsense counters stealth), but sharply limited for surface eavesdropping/sight-based stealth — good built-in roleplay tension
- **Ranger**: reflavor tracking as scent-casting rather than sight
- **Casters (Wizard/Sorcerer/Cleric/Druid)**: no mechanical obstacle — verbal components reflavored as self-generated taps/vibrations, spellbooks as grooved tactile pages
- **Bard**: fun reflavor as a felt/drummed performance that needs translation for non-Undercant audiences

## DM Discussion Points

- Tremorsense's range/tiering is intentionally more generous than a flat 30 ft. blindsight, balanced by the total blindness to ungrounded threats — worth a session-zero conversation about how flying/ship-deck/rope-bridge encounters will be handled so it isn't an accidental soft-lock either direction.
- Sonic Prosthesis is a piece of gear, not a trait — fragile (AC 10, 1 hp), fails in bad weather, delivers noise rather than words at first. Recommend treating "understanding" surface speech through it as a slow narrative/downtime progression rather than an instant fix.
- The Reckoning Wall is a good built-in backstory generator: worth asking the player whether their character left the clan-hold with a debt still open, a grudge inherited but not their own, or an oath the party's mission might let them finally close out.
